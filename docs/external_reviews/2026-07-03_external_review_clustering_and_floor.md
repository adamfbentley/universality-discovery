# Extensive Review — "Feature Geometry Is Not Universality Geometry" and the universality-discovery repository

**Scope of this review.** The paper draft (dated 21 May 2026, covering experiments 62–67), the public repository at `github.com/adamfbentley/universality-discovery` as of the current main branch (experiments through 80, plus theory notes), the documentation (`docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`, `ml_paper/CLAIMS_REGISTER.md`, `ml_paper/THEORY_minimax_floor.md`), and the source code for the simulators and pipelines. All headline numbers in the paper were cross-checked against the stored result files; the simulation and evaluation code was read directly; and an independent null analysis of the ARI ceiling was computed for this review.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The paper's central argument is valid as scoped, and its scoping is unusually careful. The four-way distinction it draws — local discriminability, density geometry, scale invariance, and physical universality — is genuinely useful, and the experiments support the headline claim: for these feature families at accessible sizes, representations can be highly discriminative (3-NN up to 97.7%) while failing to organize samples into density clusters matching universality labels (HDBSCAN ARI pinned near 0.5 across every intervention tried).

Three findings from this audit sharpen or extend that argument:

**First, the ARI ceiling is not a mysterious constant — it is an arithmetic fingerprint.** Computing reference ARI values for hypothetical partitions against the paper's evaluation labels shows that the partition {EW ∪ continuum-KPZ ∪ Eden}, {BD}, {KS}, {RD} scores **ARI = 0.498** — the observed ceiling (0.484–0.510 across all representations) to within run noise. The ceiling is exactly what one gets when two known effects hold simultaneously: BD splits off (the exp72 intrinsic-width mechanism) and EW merges with the continuum blob (the stationary gradient degeneracy). This decomposition also supplies graded targets: a representation that fixed the EW/KPZ merge but not BD would score ~0.726; perfect simulator-level separation would score 0.569 (never observed — the geometry does not even resolve simulators as density modes); class recovery is 1.0. Section 3 gives the full table. This single table would materially strengthen the paper.

**Second, the repository has substantially outrun the paper, and the strongest science is in the newer material.** The paper stops at exp67. Exp72–74 then supply the physical *mechanism* for the headline negative (BD exhibits intrinsic anomalous roughening at accessible sizes — α_local ≈ 0.23 versus α_global ≈ 0.36, intrinsic width ~5× the continuum value — and this survives a causal amplitude-normalization test), exp75–77 supply the *information-theoretic reason* (a computable Le Cam minimax resolution floor: at L ≤ 256, no estimator can resolve exponent differences below ~0.27–0.44 against adversarial corrections to scaling), and exp79 distills a clean, plausibly publishable approximation-theory lemma. The clustering paper should absorb the mechanism; the floor deserves its own paper, as the repo's own manuscript outline already plans.

**Third, the project's self-correction machinery demonstrably works, and that story belongs in the paper.** Exp69 produced a tempting false positive (effective-exponent features, ARI 0.902 under a single-seed protocol); exp71's matched five-seed sweep collapsed it to near parity with raw features. The claims register explicitly marks the falsified claim. This is the paper's own diagnostic protocol (gate 6) catching its own author — the most persuasive possible worked example for Section V.D.

Verdicts in brief: the paper's numbers are faithful to the stored results (every value spot-checked matched to reported precision); the code is sound on the points that matter for the conclusions (independent per-sample seeds, so the 97.7% kNN is not cross-validation leakage; within-class parameter randomization makes the task honestly hard); the one real physics defect — the naive KPZ discretization distorting the stationary measure, the known Lam–Shin pathology — was independently caught by the project's own exp78 stationarity gate and is already an action item; and the documentation practices (experiment log, claims register, non-claims appendix, self-documented bugs in the theory note) are above typical professional standard.

## 2. Validity of the paper's central argument

### 2.1 Reconstruction of the argument

The paper argues: (i) a deliberately demanding evaluation map is chosen (BD, Eden, and continuum KPZ must merge; EW must separate despite sharing α = 1/2); (ii) hand-built spatial and temporal features achieve high supervised/local discriminability; (iii) density clustering against the evaluation labels hits a stable ARI ceiling ≈ 0.5; (iv) the ceiling survives temporal features, coarse-graining, hierarchical peeling, a fourfold finite-size sweep, HDBSCAN parameter grids, seed blocks, and the theory-favoured height-distribution observable; (v) the kNN-graph decomposition explains it (the KPZ-labelled class is multimodal, BD disconnected at microscopic scale); (vi) positive controls show the broader method family can recover critical structure when the observable couples to it; therefore (vii) feature geometry should not be equated with universality geometry at finite size, and a diagnostic protocol is proposed.

This chain is logically sound, and each link is empirically supported. The conclusions are correctly scoped: the Non-Claims appendix explicitly disclaims the asymptotic limit, feature-family completeness, and TW convergence, and the finite-size sweep and height-feature experiments were run precisely because they are the two escapes KPZ theory most favours. Both preserved the result. That is the right order of operations for a negative-result paper.

### 2.2 The null-partition analysis: the ceiling is a fingerprint, not a wall

The paper reports the ceiling as a brute empirical fact and explains it qualitatively via the kNN graph. It can do better: the ARI value itself is nearly determined once the geometry is known. Computed for this review (n = 480; label sizes 80/240/80/80):

| Hypothetical partition | ARI vs evaluation labels |
|---|---|
| Perfect class recovery (4 clusters) | 1.000 |
| BD splits off; everything else correct (5 clusters) | 0.726 |
| Perfect simulator separation (6 clusters) | 0.569 |
| **{EW ∪ KPZ ∪ Eden}, {BD}, {KS}, {RD}** | **0.498** |
| {EW ∪ KPZ}, {BD}, {Eden}, {KS}, {RD} | 0.452 |
| {EW ∪ KPZ ∪ Eden ∪ KS}, {BD}, {RD} | 0.220 |

Three consequences. First, the observed ceiling (0.484–0.510) matches the fourth row to within run noise, and that row is exactly the structure exp64's peel diagnostic found directly (HDBSCAN on the hard subset yields {EW+KPZ+Eden} vs {BD}). The ceiling is therefore fully identified: it is the joint signature of the BD split and the EW-continuum merge, nothing more. Second, the fact that no representation ever reached 0.569 shows the geometry does not even resolve *simulators* as density modes — the continuum systems genuinely overlap — which is a stronger statement than "classes aren't recovered." Third, the table supplies graded milestones: 0.726 is the score for solving only the EW/KPZ density separation, 1.0 requires also healing BD. Exp74's collapse representation is the first move on the second front. Recommendation: add this table (or its equivalent) to Section IV or V; it converts the paper's central number from an observation into an identified quantity, and it pre-empts the referee question "is 0.5 good or bad?"

### 2.3 The one genuine confound left open: sample size, not system size

The paper varies L, T, feature families, coarse-graining scale, and clusterer parameters — but never N. HDBSCAN is a density estimator; at N = 80 per system in 6–10 standardized dimensions, density modes are data-starved, and a merged or fragmented mode structure could in principle be a small-N artifact rather than a geometric fact. The paper's strongest evidence against this reading is not HDBSCAN at all but the kNN-graph connectivity (BD disconnected at b = 1) and, in the repo's later work, the exp72 mechanism — both of which are geometric statements less sensitive to N. Still, a cheap N-sweep (hold L = 128, T = 500; N from 80 to ~500 per system) would close the hole. The exp72 mechanism predicts the ceiling persists, because the obstruction is a real geometric disconnection, not statistical noise; confirming that prediction would upgrade the claim from "HDBSCAN at N = 80 fails" to "the density geometry fails," which is what the title asserts.

### 2.4 Positive-control asymmetry

The Ising and Potts controls validate the finite-size-scaling arm of the pipeline (supervised-style exponent recovery from a known observable), not the *clustering* arm. The paper is honest that these are "sanity checks on the broader analysis pipeline," but the argument would be complete with an *unsupervised* positive control: a case where density clustering demonstrably does recover physical classes (e.g., Ising snapshots above vs below T_c, where PCA/HDBSCAN separation is essentially guaranteed). That fills the 2×2 of {supervised, unsupervised} × {works, fails} and blocks the reading "maybe this clustering stack just never works."

### 2.5 A framing caution the paper mostly already handles

At finite size, "recovering universality" is by construction unattainable — universality is an asymptotic statement, and the finite systems are demonstrably pre-asymptotic (the paper's own height moments show TW convergence incomplete; BD's effective α ≈ 0.36). The paper's real claim is about the finite-size *proxy task* and the interpretive trap of conflating it with the asymptotic one, and it says so in several places. The later floor theorem (Section 5 below) makes this precise and quantitative: worst-case identifiability of the asymptotic exponent from L ≤ 256 is essentially nil. The eventual v2 of the clustering paper should cite that result as the reason the negative was *necessary*, not merely observed.

## 3. Physics audit

### 3.1 The KPZ integrator (confirmed defect, correctly self-diagnosed)

`experiments/62_feature_space_clustering.py` integrates the KPZ equation with an Euler–Maruyama step using the naive central-difference square, `grad = (h[x+1] − h[x−1])/2`, `(λ/2)·grad²`, dt = 0.01 with substepping, dx = 1, noise `√(2D·dt)·ξ`. This is precisely the discretization known since Lam & Shin (PRE 1998) to violate the fluctuation–dissipation structure of the 1D stationary state, renormalizing the effective coupling and distorting the stationary measure. The project's own exp78 referee check independently caught the consequence: the exact stationarity identity W²_sat = L/12 is violated by the KPZ ladder by up to ~8% (≈4σ) at L = 64 with an L-dependent swing, while EW sits at a harmless constant amplitude ratio ~0.96. The experiment log correctly attributes this to the integrator, correctly notes the estimator "reads a distortion that is in the data," and correctly queues an exact-measure integrator (Lam–Shin scheme) rather than more seeds.

Impact assessment: for the clustering conclusions, the defect is close to benign and arguably even on-message — it adds non-universal, implementation-specific structure to the continuum-KPZ samples, which is exactly the category of contamination the paper argues dominates finite-size feature geometry. It matters more for (a) exp76's per-system gates, where it caused the KPZ gate failure, and (b) any statement about continuum-KPZ TW convergence rates. Recommendation: implement the improved discretization, rerun exp62/63 headline numbers once, and report that the ceiling is unchanged (the expected outcome). That single robustness sentence removes the most likely technical referee objection to the whole surface-growth benchmark.

### 3.2 The BD skewness sign (not a bug, but under-explained — and it hides a good prediction)

Table V reports BD growth-window height skewness −0.393 where the TW-GOE reference is |skew| ≈ 0.2935 and λ_BD > 0 (lateral sticking makes growth velocity increase with slope, so the signed TW prediction is *positive*). The measured value is therefore opposite in sign to the asymptotic prediction and larger in magnitude, with excess kurtosis (+0.389) also overshooting the GOE reference (0.165). The exp67 code is careful about this — its docstring states the sign convention, compares |skew|, and documents the spatial-pooling caveat — but the paper's text stops at "BD's discrete signature dominates its class."

There is a concrete mechanism available, and it unifies exp67 with exp72. The nearest-neighbour sticking rule pins deposited columns to the local neighbour maximum; columns sitting below the local envelope (unfilled holes) are common, so the site-height histogram relative to the interface envelope acquires a long *lower* tail — negative skew — at the lattice scale. Exp72 measured exactly the scale of this contamination: BD's intrinsic width √G(2) ≈ 7.07 versus ~1.4 for the continuum systems, so at L = 128 the one-point histogram is dominated by intrinsic lattice fluctuations, not by the KPZ fluctuation term whose skew is the TW signal. This yields a falsifiable prediction worth one small experiment (call it exp67b): block-average the height field over blocks comparable to the intrinsic scale (b ≈ 4–8) *before* computing growth-window moments; BD's skewness should flip sign toward the positive TW value as the intrinsic contribution is averaged out. If confirmed, Section IV F gains a mechanism and a sign-flip demonstration rather than a shrug; if refuted, that is itself interesting (it would say the contamination is not single-scale, echoing exp73). Either way, Eden's weakly negative value (−0.071) at T = 500 suggests the per-system growth-window definition deserves a check for crossover contamination at pilot scale.

### 3.3 Correct theory usage worth advertising

The paper's use of the stationary gradient degeneracy — the 1+1D KPZ stationary slope measure is Gaussian and independent of λ under periodic boundaries — is correct and correctly cited (Barabási–Stanley; Medina et al.), and it generates a successful prediction the paper undersells: the 6D EW–KPZ centroid distance should *not* grow with L, and Table IV shows it flat-to-slightly-decreasing (0.905 → 0.899 across a fourfold range). A theory-predicted null that the data then exhibit is a stronger credential than most positive results; a sentence flagging it as a passed prediction would be cheap and effective. The KS operational labeling (separate finite-size class, asymptotic KS→KPZ crossover acknowledged) and the growth-window rationale for height moments (the saturated periodic height field is a Brownian bridge, Gaussian even for KPZ) are both correct.

### 3.4 Reference verification

The paper's most recent citation, [11] Widmann et al., checks out against the published record: *Science* **392**, issue 6794 (9 April 2026) — the 2D polariton KPZ observation. Given the topic, the companion 1D result (Fontaine et al., *Nature* **608**, 687 (2022)) is a natural addition alongside it in the introduction's experimental-standard paragraph.

## 4. Statistical and code audit

**Sample independence and CV validity.** Each of the N samples per system is an independent simulation with its own seed (`seed = seed_offset + i`), and — importantly — with per-sample randomized physical parameters (ν, D, λ drawn uniformly from [0.5, 2.0]). The 5-fold kNN cross-validation therefore contains no trajectory or seed leakage; the 97.7% is real. The within-class parameter dispersion is a quiet design strength the paper never mentions: it guarantees that clusters cannot simply be parameter points, making the discriminability finding more meaningful and the clustering task honestly harder. Worth a sentence in Methods. Minor code note: the reliance on the legacy global `np.random.seed` inside numba kernels works but is fragile (parameter draws and trajectory streams interleave through one global state); migrating to per-sample `np.random.Generator` instances would make provenance airtight.

**Evaluation choices.** Standardization is fit on the pooled dataset before clustering; for the clustering metrics this is standard, and for kNN it is a negligible transductive effect at these accuracies. Reporting HDBSCAN ARI both with and without noise points (Table III) is the right call, as is the parameter sensitivity grid (non-noise ARI 0.41–0.50 across the grid — the ceiling is not a parameter accident). Heavy-tailed amplitude features (variances spanning orders of magnitude, e.g. RD's Laplacian variance) are z-scored rather than log-transformed; a log transform would make the Euclidean geometry less pathological but would not — and per exp73, demonstrably does not — remove the BD obstruction, since the split survives explicit amplitude normalization. The exp73 result licenses the paper to dismiss this whole class of objection in one sentence.

**Number verification.** Every value spot-checked matched the stored results to reported precision: exp63 (HDBSCAN 0.4963; KMeans 0.1846 → 0.4983; kNN 0.827 → 0.977; d_EW,KPZ 0.874 → 1.326), the exp66 finite-size table (e.g. L=64 row: 6D core 0.463, d 0.905, 10D core 0.482, 3-NN 0.969), and the full exp67 moment table including the TW reference constants (0.2935/0.1652 GOE; 0.2241/0.0934 GUE). The paper is a faithful report of the repository.

## 5. The post-paper arc (exp68–80): assessment

This is where the project's deepest material now lives, and the user's request for "indications of deeper insights" is best answered by evaluating it.

**Exp69 → 71 (the caught false positive).** Effective-exponent features produced ARI 0.902 under a single-seed protocol; the matched five-seed sweep collapsed the advantage to near parity with raw multi-L features (KPZ-quotient ARI ~0.17–0.19). The claims register explicitly marks the strong version of the claim "False under exp71." Two further traps of the same species were caught in exp74 (a degenerate joint (α, z) collapse fit returning z ≈ 1.0 for KPZ, and a merge metric that mistakenly included the target in its own reference group). This is the diagnostic protocol functioning as designed on its own author, and it is the single most persuasive content available for the paper's protocol section. Use it.

**Exp72–74 (the mechanism).** BD alone among the KPZ-labelled systems exhibits intrinsic anomalous roughening at accessible sizes (α_local ≈ 0.23 versus α_global ≈ 0.36, both far from 0.5; intrinsic width ~5× continuum), while Eden — discrete but not anomalous — stays merged with the continuum members. The causal test (exp73) shows normalizing out the intrinsic-width amplitude does not merge BD (outlier ratio 9.2 → 8.8; 0% co-membership in every condition), so the obstruction is a dimensionless, multi-scale *shape* property, not an amplitude. The constructive test (exp74) shows a multi-L scaling-collapse representation moves BD from outlier ratio ~9 to ~2–3, with the master-curve-shape representation putting BD's nearest neighbour at Eden — and pins the residual obstruction to a single quantity, BD's slowly-converging static α. This arc converts the paper's headline from "clustering fails" to "clustering fails *because* the dominant finite-size local signal is a measured, non-universal anomalous-roughening effect orthogonal to the class, and representations built from scaling structure partially heal it." That is a publishable mechanism, and it belongs in the clustering paper, not just the log.

**Exp75–77 (the floor theorem).** The Le Cam construction is correct in form: Gaussian model on log W_sat ladders, KL for Gaussian means, Pinsker, two-point minimax bound, with the confusion gap D²(Δα) minimized over intercept and correction nuisances on both sides. The logical direction of conservativeness is correctly reasoned — restricting the adversary to the single-power correction family means the computed floor is a valid impossibility threshold for any richer correction class containing it. The headline numbers are striking and, if they survive the pending checks, important: D²(0.1) ≈ 1.3×10⁻⁷ on the real design, so resolving Δα = 0.1 at continuum-system noise needs ~10⁵ seeds; the floor is nearly flat in seed count (identifiability-limited, not noise-limited) and decays only inverse-logarithmically in L_max — the quantitative form of "exponents need decades, not statistics." The degeneracy mechanism is cleanly identified: the exponent direction is the ω→0 boundary of the correction cone ((1 − L^{−ω})/ω → log L), so small-ω corrections *are* exponent shifts up to Taylor remainder, and the amplitude bound is the entire content of identifiability.

Open items before this is submitted, all of which the theory note itself flags (a very good sign): (a) the linearized ‖P⊥x‖ implementation is known-broken (a 40-vector ω-grid trivially spans the 7-point space) — ensure no public number ever derives from it; (b) σ is treated per system — verify whether the seed noise on log W_sat is materially L-dependent, and if so move to the heteroscedastic KL sum; (c) the 7-point-sum versus continuous-L² normalization between exp77 and the lemma needs reconciling; (d) whether the nonlinear log(1 + u·L^{−ω}) family strengthens the adversary beyond the linear-N statement; (e) the priority pass — Hall & Welsh (1984) and Drees on tail-index minimax rates are the right anchors, the Müntz–Jackson/Braess–Hackbusch/Borwein–Erdélyi corpus is the right approximation-theory neighbourhood, and the two 2026 arXiv preprints the note itself cites ("Müntz–Szász networks") must be read before any priority claim.

**Exp79 (the lemma).** E_N ≈ c_N·√T·U·(ΔαT/U)^{N+1} passed clean scale checks in N, U, Δα, and T (measured per-doubling ratios 4.16–4.75 against predicted 4 for U; 8.3–8.7 against 8 for Δα; constants c₁, c₂ pinned by certificate scripts with optimizer seeded at the construction). The proof architecture — explicit Richardson-node construction for the upper bound, annihilator operator plus Newman-type Markov inequalities for exponential sums for the lower bound — is the standard and plausible route. The note's own assessment that this is "the single mathematically substantive object the project has produced" is fair. The identification of FSS extrapolation with finite-window inverse-Laplace inversion (hence classical exponential ill-posedness, familiar from analytic continuation of imaginary-time QMC data) is the kind of framing that earns citations. The stated corollaries (seeds-versus-decades exchange rate ~2^{2N+3} per window doubling; quantitative ladder-design rule; smallest-irrelevant-eigenvalue as the resolution setter, retrodicting the log-correction pathologies at upper critical dimensions and 2D XY) are each individually worth a paragraph in the floor paper.

**Exp80.** The floor transfers to β (≈0.07–0.08, placing the EW/KPZ β-gap at the floor — consistent with why β_eff improved discriminability without density recovery) and to Ising ν, where the strict floor (0.05–0.13) brackets exp52d's 7.3% deviation. Retrodicting the project's own positive control from the theorem is a satisfying closure and should be highlighted.

## 6. Repository, notes, and documentation review

**Documentation quality: exceptional.** `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` is a genuine chronological research log across eighty experiments — with per-experiment verdicts, *counted failed predictions* (exp64: "Predictions: 2/7 passed"), and caveats recorded at the moment of the result rather than retrofitted. `ml_paper/CLAIMS_REGISTER.md` maps every claim to its evidence and explicitly marks falsified claims. The theory note documents its own implementation bug and its own pending priority checks. The paper carries a Non-Claims appendix. Collectively this is a level of epistemic hygiene rare even in professional groups, and it is the project's most transferable asset: whatever venue the papers land in, this repository is a portfolio piece for research practice in itself.

**Reproducibility.** `requirements.txt`, `pytest.ini` with smoke tests, and a CI badge are present. Experiments are mostly self-contained scripts with pilot/quick flags and JSON outputs whose paths the paper's Appendix A indexes correctly. The exp73 anchor practice — reproducing the stored exp62 ceiling exactly before running a new manipulation — is a model pattern.

**Hygiene issues (cosmetic but worth fixing before the repo gets external traffic).** Roughly fifty `results_exp70_*` directories sit at the repository root; consolidating them under `results/` (the directory already exists) would make the landing view legible. There is a stray editor workspace file (`universality discover WS.code-workspace`). More substantively, two generations of simulators coexist: the legacy `src/simulation/physics_simulation.py` (with, e.g., a thermal-noise parameter on the BD step and per-step mean-centering inside the trajectory loop) and the current in-script numba simulators in exp62/63 that everything since actually uses. Consolidating to a single source of truth — or clearly marking the legacy module as archival — prevents future experiments from silently importing the wrong physics. Finally, the README now tells a two-project story (clustering negative, then floor theorem) top-loaded with the newer result; that is the right emphasis, but the paper PDF still points readers at a repository whose headline is a different result than the paper's — a one-line "for the paper's exact scope, see tag/release X" (and cutting a release tagged at the paper's state) would keep the two artifacts coherent.

**Consistency between paper and repo.** The acknowledgments URL (`github.com/adamfbentley/universality-discovery`) is correct and public. The one substantive divergence is temporal, not factual: the paper (exp62–67) predates the mechanism (exp72–74) and the theorem (exp76–80), and several of its "Limitations" are now answered in the repo. That is an argument for a v2, not a correction.

## 7. Deeper insights and where this should go

**The arc itself is the insight.** The project's trajectory — discriminability ≠ geometry (exp62–67) → geometry failure has a measured physical mechanism (exp72–74) → the mechanism reflects an information-theoretic impossibility (exp75–77) → the impossibility is a clean approximation-theory lemma with an RG reading (exp79) — is a complete story about *why ML-assisted universality claims fail at finite size, and what it costs to fix them*. Few negative results ever get pushed to their theorem. When writing up, tell it in that order.

**"Flows, not snapshots."** The same-class exponent control (Fig. 4), exp73's dimensionless-shape finding, and exp74's collapse-representation recovery all point one direction: universality information at finite size lives in *derivatives with respect to scale* (exponents, collapse residuals, multi-scale shape), not in feature values at a scale. Exp64's multiscale experiment concatenated scales rather than differentiating across them; a per-sample representation built from the feature trajectory under coarse-graining (its slope in b, or the collapse master curve exp74 already computes) is the principled version, and exp74 shows it is the first thing that ever moved BD toward Eden. The planned exp81 observable-information hierarchy is the right formalization of this on a solvable testbed.

**The exp67 × exp72 bridge.** The BD skewness sign anomaly (Section 3.2) is currently an unexplained table entry; the intrinsic-width mechanism plus the block-averaged sign-flip prediction turns it into a demonstration. Cheap, decisive, and it connects the paper's weakest section to the repo's strongest mechanism result.

**Complete the control matrix.** Add one unsupervised positive control (Section 2.4) and the N-sweep (Section 2.3). Together with the null-ARI table (Section 2.2), these close the three referee holes this review found in the clustering paper.

**Literature to add.** For the clustering paper: Koch-Janusz & Ringel (Nat. Phys. 2018, RSMI — learning RG-relevant operators is precisely the "right observable" the paper says clustering lacks) and optionally Li & Wang (PRL 2018, neural-network RG); Fontaine et al. (Nature 608, 687 (2022)) alongside the Widmann Science paper. For the floor paper: Hall & Welsh (Ann. Statist. 1984) and Drees on tail-index minimax; Braess–Hackbusch on exponential-sum approximation; Borwein–Erdélyi for Müntz-system Markov inequalities; and the two 2026 "Müntz–Szász networks" preprints for the priority check.

**Publication strategy.** Two papers, as the manuscript outline already concludes. (1) The floor theorem + amortized estimator is the stronger and more novel contribution; Machine Learning: Science & Technology is a good fit as targeted, with SciPost Physics as an ambitious alternative if the lemma's lower bound is proven. (2) The clustering paper, revised to v2 with the mechanism (exp72–74), the null-ARI table, the exp69/71 case study, the Lam–Shin robustness rerun, and the two added controls, fits SciPost Physics Core, Phys. Rev. E, or J. Stat. Mech.; arXiv cross-list cond-mat.stat-mech + cs.LG. The diagnostic protocol is the citable object in that paper — consider giving the benchmark (six systems, evaluation map, feature families, gates) a name and a versioned release so others can run against it.
